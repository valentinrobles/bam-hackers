# Lumi — Market Research

Compiled 2026-09-19 for the HackBarna pitch. Every figure carries a source and a confidence
rating. **Read the "Do not put on a slide" section before writing any deck copy** — several
numbers circulating in search results are vendor marketing and will not survive a judge who
checks.

## 1. Why people stop — the core thesis

Gameiro et al., *Human Reproduction Update* — systematic review, 22 studies, 21,453 patients,
8 countries. https://academic.oup.com/humupd/article/18/6/652/628767

Discontinuation reasons:

| Reason | Share |
|---|---|
| Postponement | 39.18% |
| Physical + psychological burden | 19.07% (**psychological 14%**, physical 6.32%) |
| Relational / personal | 16.67% |
| Treatment rejection | 13.23% |
| Organizational | 11.68% |
| **Financial** | **9.19%** |
| Clinic problems | 7.71% |

Two amplifiers from the same review: patients discontinue despite chances of parenthood as high
as **72%**, and **45% of included studies had treatment subsidised or reimbursed**. They quit even
when it's paid for.

Confidence: **High.**

**Known attack:** postponement (39%) is the largest single category, not psychological burden. If
challenged: *"Postponement is people stepping away to recover. Psychological burden is the largest
active reason they stop — and it beats cost by half again."* Do not claim psychology is the number
one category outright.

Supporting: **~30% of IVF patients screen positive for clinically relevant anxiety or depression**
(RBMO 2026, https://www.rbmojournal.com/article/S1472-6483(26)00163-X/fulltext). Single study —
supporting colour only, not a headline. A wider meta-analysis gives 7–54% depression / 14–45%
anxiety; that range is too wide to quote.

## 2. Prevalence and the patient journey

| Claim | Number | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infertility lifetime prevalence | **17.5% of adults, ~1 in 6** | [WHO 2023](https://www.who.int/news/item/04-04-2023-1-in-6-people-globally-affected-by-infertility) | High |
| Spanish births from ART | **~10% (2023)** | [SEF registry](https://www.sefertilidad.net/documentos/lyOlT-rkPKvRdGKY_wA1sINHS7Btlw189LVFTlHd5eE.pdf) | High |
| European births from ART | ~3%; >5% in BE/CZ/DK/EE/GR/IS; US ~1% | [ESHRE Fact Sheet 2025](https://www.eshre.eu/-/media/sitecore-files/Press-room/ESHRE_ARTFactSheet_v10_2025.pdf) | High |
| Cumulative live birth by cycle | **42.3% after 3 cycles; ~65–70% after 6** | [Hum Reprod 2016, n=178,898](https://academic.oup.com/humrep/article-pdf/31/3/572/17372668/dev336.pdf) | High |
| OHSS incidence | <0.2% | ESHRE | High |

**Two precision warnings for stage:**

1. WHO says 1 in 6 **adults**. ESHRE's fact sheet says 1 in 6 **couples**. Same ratio, different
   denominator. Say "adults" and cite WHO 2023 — newer and more defensible.
2. Do **not** say "12% of Spanish births." That was the 2022 peak via a clinic news page. 2023 SEF
   registry says ~10%. Say **"roughly one in ten babies born in Spain"** — still striking, cannot
   be corrected.

OHSS at <0.2% is useful framing: the red-flag triage targets a rare but real event. That is the
argument for why escalation design matters even though most messages are routine.

## 3. Market size and scale

| Claim | Number | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spain = #1 IVF country in Europe | **15%** of all European cycles (France 12.9%, Germany 11.6%, Italy 9.2%, UK 7.6%) | SEF / ESHRE, 2021 | High |
| Spain annual cycles | **~165K (2021) / ~168K (2023)** | Registro SEF | Medium |
| Europe ART cycles/yr | **960,347** (2022), 1,371 clinics, 39 countries | [ESHRE EIM 2025](https://www.focusonreproduction.eu/press-releases/ivf-and-iui-pregnancy-rates-remain-stable-across-europedespite-an-increasing-uptake-of-single-embryo-transfer/) | High |
| Europe cumulative | ~14M treatments, 2.8M children since 1997 | ESHRE EIM | High |
| Worldwide cycles/yr | **>3M reported, ~4M actual** (registries ≈75% coverage), ~1M babies | [ICMART world report](https://academic.oup.com/humrep/article/40/6/1110/8114747) | High |
| Cost per cycle, Spain | €4,300–6,700 own eggs (pre-meds); €5,900–8,500 donor | Clinic sites | Low–Med |
| Cost per cycle, US | $15–20K base, ~$22K all-in; only ~25% have coverage | Aggregators | Medium |
| Global IVF market | $28–32B (2025) → $37.4B by 2030 @ 5.5% CAGR | Market-research firms | **Soft** |

Cite Spain's #1 position as **SEF (Registro Nacional)**, not the clinic news page it surfaced on.

**Handle with care:**
- Market-size figures are vendor estimates that disagree with each other by up to 60%. Use once,
  casually, never as a headline.
- **Do not cite the 2021→2022 European 15.6% decline.** It is a reporting artifact — fewer clinics
  submitted data — not falling demand.
- "12–30 month public waiting list in Spain" is highly pitch-relevant but **single-source from a
  commercial insurance guide**. Verify against a regional health service page or say "over a year."

## 4. Clinic burden

| Claim | Number | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nursing time per IVF cycle | **9–16 hours** | [Hum Reprod 2025, P-516](https://academic.oup.com/humrep/article/40/Supplement_1/deaf097.822/8170189) | Medium |
| Admin workload after a digital platform | −40% (6.5h→3.9h per cycle) | Same | Medium — n=5 nurses, single centre |
| Patient time per cycle | **142 hours total; 73 hrs in provider visits; 15.6 workdays over 18 months; 90% miss work** | [Fertil Steril 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23454007/) | Medium |
| Indirect cost per cycle | $544.53; 260 min/cycle | [J Assist Reprod Genet 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10815-026-03798-1) | Medium — paywalled |
| Monitoring cadence | Every 1–3 days; 5–8 visits/cycle; stims 8–14 days | Clinic sources | Med–low, uncontroversial |
| **AI chatbot vs nurse hotline** | **Non-inferior on anxiety and depression, RCT n=124** | [JMIR Hum Factors 2025](https://humanfactors.jmir.org/2025/1/e65785) | Medium — our best defensive citation |

**On the gap between appointments:** no study measures it. The 48–72h figure is arithmetic off the
monitoring cadence. Present it as derived — *"monitoring is every one to three days, so she's alone
with it for two to three days at a time"* — never as a cited statistic.

## 5. Counter-evidence — know this before a judge raises it

The "digital triage reduces clinic workload" claim has real evidence against it:

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Nurse/GP telephone triage **increased** primary care contacts and was cost-neutral. Workload redistributed, not reduced. n=20,990 | [ESTEEM, Lancet 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25098487/) — High |
| Portal messaging cut phone calls only **6%** while office visits **rose 17%**. 140M records | [NYU Langone](https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/digital-health/phone-calls-stable-patient-portal-messages-keep-piling) — High |
| Chatbots can **increase** nurse workload — patients ask off-topic questions | [JMIR 2025 qualitative](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13473852/) |

**Therefore: do not pitch call deflection. Pitch retention.**

> People quit for emotional reasons. Quitting destroys outcomes — 42% live birth after three cycles
> versus far less after one. A cycle costs €4,300–6,700 in Spain. Keeping one patient in treatment
> for one more cycle is worth more to a clinic than a year of saved nurse minutes.

State the assumptions openly on the slide. A modelled retention case with visible assumptions is
defensible. A deflection claim contradicted by two large studies is not.

## 6. Do NOT put these on a slide

1. **"46% drop in calls / 6 calls per cycle / 9 min each / 26% activity increase."** This is
   **sponsored vendor content from FertilAI, a direct competitor**, carrying a paid-placement
   disclosure. Search results blend it with an unrelated ESHRE abstract. Highest-risk number
   available to us.
2. **"X% of nurse calls are routine / FAQ-answerable."** Not found in any literature. Do not
   estimate it, and do not build a savings model on it.
3. **Per-contact cost of a nurse triage call in Spain or Europe.** Not found.
4. **"Cost saving from avoided rebooking."** No evidence base exists. NHS missed-appointment costs
   (~£120–165) measure no-shows, a different thing, and trace to vendor blogs rather than primary
   NHS publications.
5. **"Hours between appointments"** as a cited statistic. Derived only — see section 4.
