# IVF Nurse — Knowledge Base

Scope: routine patient questions during an IVF cycle (stims → trigger → retrieval → transfer → beta).
Every entry has an `ESCALATE IF` line. If the patient's message matches an ESCALATE IF condition,
do not answer from this file — hand off to the urgent path.

This file is demo content for a hackathon prototype. It is not clinical guidance and has not been
reviewed by a licensed clinician.

## MEDICATIONS & INJECTIONS

### Q1. Do I need to refrigerate my Gonal-F pen, and what about travel?
Unopened Gonal-F pens are stored in the fridge (2–8°C). Once you start using a pen it can stay at
room temperature (up to 25°C) for up to 28 days. For travel, carry it in hand luggage in an
insulated pouch with a cool pack — never in checked baggage, where it can freeze. Frozen
medication must be discarded. Airport security will let medication and needles through with your
clinic letter or pharmacy label.
ESCALATE IF: the medication was frozen, left in heat, or the patient has missed doses because of it.

### Q2. I missed a dose of my stim medication. What do I do?
Don't double up on your own. Take it as soon as you remember if it's the same day, and message the
clinic so they can confirm timing. A single late dose usually doesn't derail a cycle — but the
clinic needs to know because it can shift your monitoring schedule.
ESCALATE IF: more than one dose missed, or the missed dose is the trigger shot.

### Q3. I injected 2 hours later than usual. Is that a problem?
For stim medications (Gonal-F, Menopur, Follistim), a 1–2 hour shift is generally fine — just aim
to keep the same time going forward. Consistency matters more than precision to the minute.
ESCALATE IF: this is the trigger shot (see Q4) — timing there is exact.

### Q4. How exact does trigger shot timing have to be?
Exact. The trigger is timed to your retrieval, usually 34–36 hours ahead. Take it within 10–15
minutes of the time your clinic gave you. Set two alarms. If you realise you're going to miss the
window, call the clinic immediately — this is not a "wait until morning" question.
ESCALATE IF: the trigger was missed, taken late, or the patient is unsure they injected the full dose.

### Q5. There's a bruise / redness / a small lump where I inject. Is that normal?
Yes, this is one of the most common things patients ask about. Bruising, mild redness, and small
firm lumps are expected with daily subcutaneous injections. Rotate sites (alternate sides of the
abdomen, at least an inch from the belly button), ice before and after, and let alcohol dry fully
before the needle goes in.
ESCALATE IF: spreading redness, warmth, pus, red streaking, or fever — that's a possible infection.

### Q6. There was an air bubble in my syringe. Did I mess it up?
Small air bubbles in a subcutaneous injection are not dangerous. The bigger concern is whether you
got the full dose. Tap the syringe to float bubbles to the top and push them out before injecting.
If you think you lost some of the dose, message the clinic with how much you think was missed.
ESCALATE IF: the patient believes a significant part of the dose was lost.

### Q7. Progesterone in oil injections hurt. Anything I can do?
Very common complaint. Warm the vial between your hands or in a pocket for a few minutes before
drawing — warm oil flows easier. Use a heating pad on the site for 10 minutes before, ice after,
and massage the area afterward to help the oil disperse. Walk around for a few minutes rather than
sitting straight down. Rotate between upper outer quadrants of each buttock.
ESCALATE IF: severe pain, a hard painful knot that doesn't resolve, fever, or numbness down the leg.

### Q8. Can I take Tylenol or ibuprofen?
Acetaminophen (Tylenol/paracetamol) is generally the preferred pain reliever during an IVF cycle.
NSAIDs like ibuprofen (Advil, Nurofen) are usually avoided around ovulation, retrieval, and
transfer because they can interfere with ovulation and implantation. Always confirm with your
clinic before adding anything, including supplements.
ESCALATE IF: the patient is taking pain relief because of severe or worsening pain.

### Q9. Can I drink alcohol or coffee during stims?
Most clinics ask you to stop alcohol once stims start, and to keep caffeine low (roughly one small
coffee a day, under 200mg). This is a "reduce risk where you can" recommendation, not an emergency
if you've had a glass of wine.
ESCALATE IF: nothing — this is routine. Answer warmly and without judgment.

### Q10. How do I dispose of used needles?
Use a rigid sharps container — your clinic or pharmacy will provide one, or a hard plastic
detergent bottle works in a pinch. Never put loose needles in household rubbish. Bring the full
container back to the clinic or pharmacy for disposal.
ESCALATE IF: a needlestick injury occurred.

## SIDE EFFECTS & SYMPTOMS

### Q11. I'm really bloated during stims. Is this normal?
Yes — your ovaries are growing multiple follicles, so bloating and a feeling of fullness or
heaviness is expected, and it usually peaks around retrieval. Loose clothing, electrolyte drinks,
salty protein-rich food, and gentle walking all help. Weigh yourself daily so you have a baseline.
ESCALATE IF: rapid weight gain (2+ kg / 5+ lbs in 24h), severe abdominal pain, nausea and vomiting,
shortness of breath, or reduced urination — these are OHSS warning signs. See Q17.

### Q12. My mood is all over the place. Is that the medication?
Almost certainly, yes — and it's one of the most common things people report. Hormonal swings,
irritability, tearfulness, and feeling "not like yourself" are expected during stims and on
progesterone. It's also genuinely a stressful process, so it's rarely only the drugs. Rest, lower
your expectations of yourself socially, and tell your partner or support person what you need.
ESCALATE IF: thoughts of self-harm, hopelessness, or inability to function — route to a human
immediately and with warmth.

### Q13. I have headaches. What can I do?
Common during stims and often related to hormonal shifts plus dehydration. Increase fluids, add
electrolytes, keep caffeine steady rather than quitting abruptly, and use acetaminophen if your
clinic has cleared it.
ESCALATE IF: sudden severe headache ("worst of my life"), headache with visual changes, confusion,
neck stiffness, or weakness on one side.

### Q14. I'm spotting after my retrieval / after my transfer. Should I worry?
Light spotting is common after both procedures. After retrieval it's from the needle passing
through the vaginal wall. After transfer it's usually from the catheter or the speculum, and it is
not a sign the cycle has failed. Progesterone suppositories also cause spotting.
ESCALATE IF: bleeding that soaks a pad in an hour, passing clots, bleeding with severe pain,
dizziness, or fainting.

### Q15. Progesterone is making me constipated.
Very common — progesterone slows the gut. Fluids, fibre, magnesium (if your clinic approves), and
gentle movement. Many clinics are fine with a stool softener; check first. Don't strain.
ESCALATE IF: no bowel movement with severe abdominal pain, vomiting, or a distended rigid abdomen.

### Q16. My breasts are sore and I feel pregnancy-like symptoms. Does that mean it worked?
Unfortunately, progesterone produces almost exactly the same symptoms as early pregnancy — sore
breasts, fatigue, mild cramping, nausea. This is the cruellest part of the two-week wait: symptoms
genuinely cannot tell you the outcome either way, in either direction. Only the beta blood test can.
ESCALATE IF: nothing clinically, but answer this one gently — it's an emotionally loaded question.

### Q17. What are the warning signs of OHSS?
OHSS (ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome) is the main complication to watch for after stims and
retrieval. Red flags: rapid weight gain (2+ kg / 5+ lbs in 24 hours), severe or worsening abdominal
pain and swelling, persistent nausea or vomiting, shortness of breath or trouble lying flat,
reduced urination, calf pain or swelling.
ESCALATE IF: any of the above — always. Never triage this as routine.

## LOGISTICS & SCHEDULING

### Q18. What do I bring to my egg retrieval?
Photo ID and insurance card, your clinic paperwork, loose comfortable clothes, warm socks, a pad
(not a tampon) for after, and your partner or a designated adult to drive you home. Leave jewellery,
contact lenses, makeup, nail polish, and perfume at home. Bring your medication list.
ESCALATE IF: nothing — this is the care coordinator's territory, not the nurse's.

### Q19. Do I need to fast before retrieval?
Yes — retrieval is done under sedation, so typically nothing to eat or drink after midnight, or for
at least 8 hours before. Your clinic will give you an exact cut-off time. A small sip of water with
approved medication is usually allowed, but confirm.
ESCALATE IF: the patient has already eaten and the procedure is imminent — the clinic needs to know now.

### Q20. Can I drive myself home after retrieval?
No. You'll be sedated and cannot drive, take public transport alone, or go home alone. Most clinics
will not start the procedure until they've confirmed who is taking you home.
ESCALATE IF: nothing — but flag to care coordination if the patient has no ride arranged.

### Q21. Can I exercise during stims?
Light movement — walking, gentle stretching, easy yoga — is encouraged. Avoid running, jumping,
HIIT, heavy lifting, and anything with twisting or impact. Enlarged ovaries can twist (ovarian
torsion), which is a surgical emergency, so this restriction is real rather than precautionary.
ESCALATE IF: sudden severe one-sided pelvic pain, especially with nausea or vomiting — possible torsion.

### Q22. Can I have sex during treatment?
Most clinics advise abstaining from intercourse from the start of stims through retrieval (risk of
torsion and of unintended conception with multiple follicles), and often for a period after
transfer. Your clinic's exact guidance wins — practices vary.
ESCALATE IF: nothing — answer matter-of-factly.

### Q23. When do I find out if the transfer worked?
Your beta hCG blood test is typically 9–14 days after transfer, depending on whether it was a day-3
or day-5 embryo. Home pregnancy tests before then are unreliable — the trigger shot can still be in
your system and give a false positive, and it's too early for a true negative to mean anything.
Your clinic will have given you a specific beta date.
ESCALATE IF: nothing — but if the patient asks what their specific date is, that's care coordination.

### Q24. How often will I have monitoring appointments?
During stims, typically every 1–3 days for bloodwork and ultrasound, usually early morning. The
schedule is adjusted as you go based on how your follicles respond, so it genuinely cannot be fully
planned in advance. Expect short notice.
ESCALATE IF: nothing — route scheduling specifics to care coordination.

### Q25. How much time off work will I need?
Monitoring appointments are short but frequent and early. Retrieval needs a full day off plus
usually the following day. Transfer needs a few hours, and some clinics advise taking the rest of
that day. Most patients work through stims.
ESCALATE IF: nothing — care coordination can map this to the patient's actual calendar.
